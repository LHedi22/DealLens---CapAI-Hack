Series: Series A Round | Ask: $5,000,000 | Sector: AgriTech | Geography: North America

COMPANY OVERVIEW
RoboFarm builds autonomous agricultural robots for specialty crop harvesting in North America. Our RoboFarm Harvester is a wheeled autonomous robot that uses computer vision to identify, assess, and harvest strawberries, blueberries, and peppers — crops that have historically resisted automation due to their irregular shapes and delicate nature. We are the only commercially deployed autonomous harvesting system for specialty crops in the United States with a provable field track record.

Founded: 2021 | Headquarters: Salinas, California, USA | Team Size: 34

PROBLEM
North American specialty crop farming faces an existential labor crisis. Seasonal agricultural labor is declining 9% year-over-year due to visa restrictions, aging workforce demographics, and competition from other industries. H-2A visa costs run $15-22 per worker-hour. Growers are leaving crops unharvested — $2.8 billion in specialty crops were left in fields in 2023 (USDA Economic Research Service). Each RoboFarm deployment can replace 20-30 seasonal pickers at a cost equivalent to $3.40/hour of harvesting time. Strawberry picking robots specifically have failed commercially for 15 years due to insufficient accuracy — until now.

SOLUTION
RoboFarm Harvester delivers:
- Computer vision system: custom-trained vision model (94.3% berry identification accuracy in field conditions, independently verified by UC Davis AgriTech Lab)
- Autonomous navigation: RoboFarm navigates existing row crop configurations without infrastructure modification
- Selective harvesting: real-time ripeness assessment — harvests only fruit meeting client's color/firmness spec
- Yield analytics: per-row, per-day harvest data delivered to farm management dashboard
- Fleet management: farm operators manage multiple robots from iPad-based dashboard with real-time status and alerts
- Harvest-as-a-Service: RoboFarm deploys, operates, and maintains the hardware fleet — growers pay per pound harvested ($0.08-$0.12/pound depending on crop)

ESG PROFILE
Environmental: RoboFarm robots are fully electric, charged by solar panels co-located on farm. No diesel. Each robot deployed replaces the fuel consumption of farm vehicles and crew transport associated with 20-30 human pickers. We calculate and publish our annual carbon impact per deployment — 2024 estimate: 84 tonnes CO2e avoided per 10-robot farm deployment vs. H-2A alternative. Our vision system selectively harvests only ripe fruit, reducing harvest waste by an estimated 12% vs. human crews who cannot assess ripeness as consistently at speed. We submit annual environmental impact report to our major client Driscoll's as part of their sustainability supplier program.
Social: We acknowledge that agricultural automation raises workforce transition concerns. RoboFarm creates skilled technician jobs (robot deployment, maintenance, fleet monitoring) at $28-$42/hour — significantly above H-2A picker wages ($15-$18/hour). We partner with Salinas Valley Community College to run a certified agricultural robotics technician training program for displaced farmworkers. 4 graduates of this program are now employed by RoboFarm. We also note that the H-2A program has documented exploitation risks for migrant workers — our technology reduces dependence on a labor system with documented welfare concerns.
